
Exploring fitness traits tradeoffs and microbial life history strategies using genome-scale data

Identifying microbial life-history strategies and trait tradeoffs under environmental change is argued to improve our understanding of microbial processes in ecosystem functioning. In this work, we explore microbial life strategies and related fitness-traits tradeoffs of grassland and shrubland soil microbial communities from the Loma Ridge Global Change Experiment in Southern California using the R tool Microtrait. Microtrait uses hidden Markov models to estimate fitness traits from metagenome-assembled genomes (MAGs). We ran Microtrait with 500 MAGs and derived 100 putative functional guilds according to their similarity in trait distributions. The fitness traits derived from Microtrait suggest that the putative guilds mainly invest in resource acquisition traits for substrate breakdown and transport of metabolites into the cell as well as in stress-tolerance traits, possibly in response to drought and high temperatures. The results of this work will be used to parameterize a trait-based model to explore the effects of these microbial life strategies on soil functions such as litter decomposition and soil respiration. Additionally, our approach using genome-scale data opens up new possibilities for data-driven model development that could ultimately strengthen our understanding of microbial processes and ecosystem functioning.
