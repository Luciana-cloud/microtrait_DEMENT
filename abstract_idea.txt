
Exploring fitness traits tradeoffs and microbial life history strategies using genome scale data

Identifying microbial life story strategies and the corresponding trait tradeoffs under a changing environment is argued to improve our understanding of the impacts of microbial processes on ecosystem functioning. In this work, we explore fitness trait tradeoffs and the related microbial life strategies of grassland and shrubland soil microbial communities from the Loma Ridge Global Change Experiment in Southern California using the R tool Microtrait. Microtrait uses hidden Markov models to estimate fitness traits from metagenome-assembled genomes (MAGs). We ran Microtrait with 500 MAGs and derived 100 putative functional guilds according to their similarity in trait distributions. The fitness traits derived from Microtrait suggest that the putative guilds mainly invest in resource acquisition traits for substrate breakdown and transport of metabolites into the cell as well as in stress-tolerant traits, possibly as a response to drought and high temperatures in the area. The results of this work will later be used to parameterize a trait-based model to explore the effects of the found microbial life strategies on soil functions like litter decomposition and soil respiration. Additionally, our results could point out potential model development of trait-based models based on data that could ultimately strengthen our understanding of microbial processes and ecosystem functioning.
